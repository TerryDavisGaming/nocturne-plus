nocturne+ 2.9.1 package verification
checked october 3, 2026 on windows 11, using windows powershell 5.1, on
nocturne 1.0.2 (steam build 25684815).

the dlls
- both were built from the package's source (crlf) with -c Release,
  against the interop assemblies bepinex and melonloader 0.7.3 generated
  from the updated game.
- payload/NocturnePlus.dll
  b4c7c29577a0b1d6c76bdfaa99091c3bb243732a3665a3f6103f6b644841403c
  payload/NocturnePlus.MelonLoader.dll
  18133fb2c897e944857bdd02fb509593ec906b41f6c7a96d936d41861096a943
- the package's source rebuilds into the same two dlls, byte for byte.
- the installer also takes the published 2.9.0 dlls (479573ba... and
  7338b2bb...), the published 2.8.0 dlls (802e8a37... and e4379acc...)
  and the first 2.8.0 test build's (9caae1b5... and bef23f14...) as
  copies to upgrade.
- both release dlls were started in the game, one loader at a time:
  "Nocturne+ 2.9.1 loaded" and the title's version text, with no error
  or exception in the log.

the installer
109 checks were planned against throwaway copies of the game files, with
every registry write blocked: 89 passed, none failed, and 20 were skipped
(below). the real game folder, its plugin and mods folders, the
melonloader sandbox and the game's preferences key were unchanged
afterwards. the game files are the updated ones: the installer's pins (the
game code, its metadata, and two of the original layout files) are the
1.0.2 build's.
- every package script parses, the installer's pins match the payload, and
  SHA256SUMS.txt matches every file.
- a bepinex install with the bundled loader, its -whatif preview, a repeat
  install that changes nothing, melonloader installs and refusals, loader
  switching, doorstop conflicts, uninstall and its preview, and a declined
  confirmation.
- a wrong steam build and a game file the installer doesn't know are
  refused.
- upgrading from the published 2.9.0 installs 2.9.1 and keeps the old dll
  as a backup, on both loaders (the published 2.9.0 package's own dlls).
- upgrading from the published 2.8.0 installs 2.9.1 and keeps the old dll
  as a backup, on both loaders.
- upgrading from nocturne but better 2.1.2, 2.5.0, 2.6.1, 2.6.2 and 2.7.0
  turns the old copy off and keeps it as .disabled-, on both loaders (2.1.2
  had no melonloader build), and the preview names it.
- 20 checks were skipped. 16 are upgrades from 8 older nocturne but better
  versions (2.2.0, the 2.3.0 test build, 2.3.0, 2.4.0, 2.4.1, both 2.5.0
  test builds and 2.6.0, each for two loaders) whose dlls were gone from the
  test pc. the other 4 set a game up with the 2.7.0 or 2.8.0 package's own
  installer first; those installers only accept game 1.0.1's files and
  refuse the updated ones, so the checks can't run on them. the same
  upgrades from those versions' dlls ran as the checks above.
- unknown dlls at the new or the old paths are refused.
- the steam library lookups (fake steam folders and registry values), both
  fullscreen fix scripts, odd temp folders, and install.cmd and
  uninstall.cmd from a folder with an odd name.
- uninstall looks for potato's saved settings only through the guard, which
  finds no key in a test copy. the step itself ran on a test registry key
  only.

the fullscreen fix, on the real game file
the script's pins for build 25684815 were checked with -WhatIf, then the
fix was enabled on the test pc's own globalgamemanagers: the file went
from the pinned original to the pinned patched hash, the game started on
direct3d 11 (it starts on direct3d 12 without the fix), and restoring put
the original back byte for byte. the backup copy the test made was then
removed, so the game folder was as it was found.

gameplay coverage
both loaders were tested in-game with qa builds: the release source plus
test hooks, on nocturne 1.0.2 with bepinex 6.0.0-be.788 and melonloader
0.7.3. every run took a snapshot of the player's plugin, settings and
saves first and put it back afterwards. no run changed a story .sav file.
the sweep below ran on the final source, except three bepinex runs (quick
save, note colors, performance): see the note after the list.
per loader:
- custom battle dialogue: 37 of 37 checks and 8 of 8 after-run checks with
  the lines going on by themselves, and 26 of 26 and 8 of 8 with real enter
  presses for the lines that wait for a key. a battle with lines before the
  fight, during it (including lines that stop the song) and after a win was
  played twice, and one battle was loaded from a file. the first run on
  2.9.0's code, on the same game (bepinex only), passed 12 and failed 25:
  the update renamed the compiler's lambda that the dialogue's end-of-battle
  hook looked up by name, so the dialogue wasn't installed.
- main menu music: 17 of 17 checks, and 18 of 18 when the game starts with
  the setting already saved as off. continuing into the story with it off
  passed 4 of 4: the story's music started at the same moment as in a
  control run with the setting on (the control ran on bepinex). the music
  state, the playing segment and the beat callbacks were read from the
  game's audio engine; no sound was recorded.
- your own note colors: 33 of 33 checks (31 as in 2.9.0 plus two for
  akuma in the note colors row). the game's new note preview shows under
  the mod's custom note colors row, and the screenshots show no overlap.
- performance: 30 of 30 checks in the menus and the story, and 12 of 12
  in battles.
- clicks through the chart editor and the battle creator: 10 of 10.
- quick save slots: 31 of 31 on bepinex only, with real key presses. the
  check for a slot from another playthrough used slot 11, because f7 never
  reached the game on the test pc: a check found f7, f8, f9 and f12
  already registered as global hotkeys by another program there.
note on those three bepinex runs: they passed on the source from before the
last small edits (the version number, comments, one error message and some
wording) but not when repeated on the final build in the evening: keys and
clicks didn't reach the game, which switched to mouse mode and lost its
selection. the published 2.9.0 code failed the same checks in the same way
on the same pc in the same hour, and the melonloader runs of palettes and
modes passed on the final build, so it was the test pc, not the code.
one melonloader run of the start-with-music-off check also lost its selection
once (a key navigation check) and passed 18 of 18 when repeated.
the update rewrote 36 of the game's audio banks. the music table the mod
uses (research/music/musicmap.py, which maps the game's songs to their
audio in the banks) was rebuilt from the new banks and is byte for byte the
same as the one made from the old banks, and every audio source the mod's
table points at (24 inside banks, 107 loose files) still has the same
position, size and format.
the hub's server code: 185 deno tests passed (163 on 2.9.0's server code);
the two for a key the owner refused a picture of fail on the previous
code. the pictures change was tested against stand-ins, not the live hub.
not tried in the game: akuma's colors by controller button in battle, quick
save on melonloader, dialogue lines after a loss and skipping lines by
holding a key, the hit and miss sound levels on the rewritten sound
effects bank, the installer on a real game folder, and the two latency
test fixes (checked in the game's code). uninstall's step for potato's
settings ran on a test registry key.

nocturne but better 2.7.0 - package verification
checked september 27, 2026 on windows 11, using windows powershell 5.1.

installer
83 checks ran against throwaway copies of the game files. the
real game folder was not used, and its files were unchanged afterwards.
- every package script parses in windows powershell 5.1.
- install previews (-whatif) leave the game folder unchanged for both
  loaders, and so does the uninstall preview.
- a game without melonloader gets the bundled bepinex and the plugin. a
  repeat install changes nothing.
- upgrading from the published 2.1.2, 2.2.0, 2.4.1, 2.5.0, 2.6.0, 2.6.1,
  or 2.6.2 plugin installs 2.7.0 and keeps the old dll as a backup.
  upgrading the 2.2.0, 2.4.1, 2.5.0, 2.6.0, 2.6.1, or 2.6.2 melonloader
  mod does the same.
- finding the game without being told where: fake steam folders (never
  the real registry) list a library on a missing drive, an offline network
  share, folders that are gone, names with [brackets], spaces and letters
  like ü, steam's old library format, and locked or unreadable steam files.
  each finds exactly the one test game, and an install and uninstall work
  that way. these checks fail on 2.6.1's installer, which stopped with
  "cannot find drive" or missed the library. the fullscreen fix script
  gets the same lookup checks.
- install.cmd and uninstall.cmd work from a folder with [brackets], an
  apostrophe, unicode, & and %.
- with melonloader 0.7.3 installed and turned on, the mod goes into mods and
  the bepinex copy is disabled. a melonloader-only game gets no bepinex
  files. a repeat install changes nothing.
- after melonloader is removed, running the installer again brings back the
  bepinex plugin and disables the mods copy. only one copy is ever enabled.
- a melonloader that is turned off in userdata\loader.cfg, or whose proxy
  dll is missing, leaves bepinex in charge.
- a stray folder on the unused loader's path doesn't block the other loader.
- uninstall disables every enabled copy for both loaders, keeps the loaders,
  and repeats cleanly. declining its confirmation is reported as incomplete.
- the installer refuses these without changing any file: an unknown dll in
  either mod location, a corrupt payload, melonloader older than 0.7.3, a
  melonloader 0.5 layout, bepinex beside an enabled melonloader, melonloader
  when it isn't installed or won't start, a different steam build, an
  unknown game asset, a conflicting loader binary, and a disabled doorstop
  configuration. an existing compatible doorstop configuration is kept.

for 2.7.0 the installer only changes its version and pinned dll hashes
since 2.6.2, and the optional fullscreen script is unchanged. the real
installer was not run on the test pc's own game this time (2.6.2's
was). neither script touches the custom charts, custom battles, arcade
gear or hub files.

shareable dlls
- both are built from the 2.7.0 source with debug symbols disabled and no
  git commit hash, so they contain no development-machine paths.
- this package's source folder, built with -c Release, gave the same
  bytes as the shipped dlls. the bytes depend on the files' line endings,
  so build from the folder as it is, without converting them.
- the source folder has all of the mod's code, including its art\,
  audio\ and hub\ folders. the online hub's server isn't in the
  package; it's in the server folder of the github repository.

driven from the game: a typed path imported a pack, a typed name exported
one, and a cancelled dialog changed nothing. charts with a note row of the
wrong width, with no song, or for a split boss fight were refused with the
reason; a lowercase song name matched the game's; a missing ';' didn't
break the next tag. "write game charts" wrote 177 charts, all 941 of their
difficulties passed the chart check, and one imported. with a custom chart
picked, the firefly battle played it on its melody, and the score the game
records under "Firefly - 1" went to the custom chart's own key instead.
the player's saves were restored and checked by hash after each run.
the custom charts page opened from the main menu and from the difficulty
screen's custom entry and showed only its six rows, and the gameplay page
got its own rows back. the chart editor was driven with real key presses
and mouse clicks (filtering songs, picking a melody and a starting chart,
play/pause, snap, speed, tools, placing and deleting a note, drawing a hold,
select all, mirror, undo, naming), escape never reached the menus under it,
and back worked again after it closed. its firefly music matched a
reference render to the sample, and a chart it saved played in the firefly
battle. the redesigned editor was then tested on a 5120x2160 screen on
both loaders: every tab, a saved chart with two scroll speed sections and a
bookmark, an export pack holding the notes, events and scroll speeds, and
real clicks on its tabs and a key rebound through the keys tab and used.
in battle the notes moved at half and double speed in those sections and
at normal speed elsewhere. on a song with enemy events a saved chart kept
all 7 of the song's events, and an edited event was saved with the chart
and read by the game. a chapter button turned gold with its sparkles once
every card in the chapter showed theirs.

earlier features: with the game's auto-play on every lane, the firefly battle
ran through the circle and arrow skins in all three scroll modes, receptor
heights from -10 to +30, note sizes from 70% to 150%, and lane spacings from
70% to 150%, with note flares off and on and enemy attack opacity at 0%,
30%, and 60%. going back to the default layout restored all 70 values the
mod touches. the enemy faded during its attacks and came back after, and
flares were hidden only while the setting was off. the timing bar got
made-up hits, because auto-played hits are left off it. on bepinex, a run
without auto-play pressed the lanes through the game's own input check.
the hit sound and the miss sound were recorded from the pc's output at
several volumes and came out at the set levels. options > gameplay showed
the nine rows and reset them, options > audio showed the four sound rows,
and the title screen and the intro's logo card showed the new text. for
2.4.0, a script sent real key presses to the battle's ready screen: tab,
windows, and alt, alone, with another key, and pressed and released inside
one frame, were all ignored, and j started the fight. the note colors
preview was checked in all six palettes with all three skins.
real key presses, five-lane charts, and enemies other than the firefly
have not been tried with those settings.
